## Measure visual discrimination and choice response speed

### What is Choice Reaction Time?

Choice reaction time requires identifying a specific stimulus and selecting the matching response action among multiple options.

Unlike simple reaction tests with a single trigger, choice tasks engage sensory identification, cognitive spatial mapping, and motor selection.

### How the Test Works

The directional test presents 10 trials featuring arrow stimuli facing Up, Down, Left, or Right.

Respond as quickly and accurately as possible using corresponding arrow keys or on-screen directional buttons.

- 10 discrete stimulus trials
- Four cardinal directions randomized per trial
- Foreperiod delay between 1000ms and 3000ms

### Speed and Accuracy Balance

Performance is evaluated across both mean response time on correct choices and overall directional accuracy percentage.

### Scope and Testing Factors

Physical keyboard layout, key switch travel, and touch responsiveness affect latency. This assessment serves as an educational benchmark and is not a clinical diagnostic measure.

### Frequently Asked Questions

#### What does the Directional Choice Test measure?

It benchmarks choice reaction time and visual discrimination. Unlike simple visual detection, you must identify target orientation and initiate the matching directional motor output.

#### Why is choice reaction time slower than simple reaction time?

Choice tasks require cognitive cortical discrimination (Hick-Hyman Law) before motor initiation, typically adding 100ms to 180ms compared to simple visual cues.

#### What input method is recommended for directional choice benchmarking?

A physical keyboard with dedicated arrow keys provides the lowest polling jitter and clearest spatial mapping compared to touchscreens.

#### What counts as an invalid trial in the direction test?

Responses occurring before stimulus presentation or under 80.0ms are flagged as false starts, while trials exceeding 3000ms are marked as timed out.
